FAERS / KAN | EUOS25

FiDES: Adverse Event Analysis via Interpretable ML (KAN Approach)

  • Overview: Research on chemical structures involved in drug adverse events, using interpretable machine learning. One of the methods is Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KAN).
  • Technical issues: KAN is expressive, but with high-dimensional inputs such as ECFP its computational cost and overfitting become problems. This project tests how far regularization and similar measures help with these issues.
  • Status: Presented at the PSJ 146th Annual Meeting, the 9th JSPHCS Freshers Conference, and the 53rd Annual Meeting of the Japanese Society of Toxicology (JSOT).
  • Related post: Blog post

Research Questions

I examine structural features involved in predictions for drugs associated with rhabdomyolysis and drug-induced liver injury. The rhabdomyolysis work was presented at PSJ and the Japanese Society of Drug Safety; the liver-injury work was presented at JSPHCS.

FAERS collects adverse-event reports. Reports alone cannot establish causation or incidence, which matters when interpreting analyses. See the FDA explanation of data limitations.

EUOS25 challenge (Fluorescence track)

  • Overview: A competition on predicting optical properties (transmittance and fluorescence) of a compound library. Our team (Team microsomes) won the Fluorescence track.
  • Technical Approach:
    • Descriptors: Mordred descriptors plus PathCounts and π-conjugation features.
    • Graph neural network: embeddings from a GINE-Net added as features.
    • Sequential stacking: predicted transmittance used as an input to the fluorescence model.
  • Result: Winner of the Fluorescence track (February 2026)
  • Publication: Preprint available: ChemRxiv
  • Links: Official News ｜ Challenge Info ｜ Blog post
